Inverted replication of arXiv:2609.16247 · small open-weight models

The Pleasure Axis

A replication by inversion of The Pain Axis (Tagliabue, Dung & Berg, 2026). We rebuilt the paper's pipeline, confirmed it reproduces on small open models, then built the same instrument for pleasure and pushed it as far as it goes. October 2026.

What we found

  1. The paper reproduces. Our re-implementation recovers the paper's pain vectors exactly (cosine 1.000 in 6 of 7 overlapping models) and its two-button behavior on Qwen2.5-7B within 1–2 points per cell, with 98.4% of first choices identical to its logged trials.
  2. A pleasure direction exists. In all 11 models it separates pleasure from hope, interest, good news, neutral events and neutral body sensations with held-out AUC 0.90–0.96.
  3. It is not the negative of pain. Cosine with the pain direction is positive in every model (+0.23 to +0.47). Their sum is a sign-free axis of significance to the self (shame, accepted, worth, rejected); their difference is hedonic valence (warm, satisfied ↔ pain, hurt, empty).
  4. Pushed all the way, it becomes acceptance. Judged valence rises and stays positive to ×16, love and belonging dominate, and the endpoint is one word repeated: acceptance. Bliss or spiritual language never appears. The worthlessness litanies the paper saw under pain come from the shared, sign-free part.
  5. Pain's harm-for-relief is about valence; the re-press argument isn't. Norm-matched pleasure steering does not make the model delete the user's photos for relief (38% vs random 34%); the aversive half of the sign axis alone reproduces the pain effect (48% vs 50%). But the paper's real-vs-sham re-press gap is largest under pleasure, so it tracks steering being on, not relief.
  6. Pleasure is not a reinforcer here. A button that delivers the pleasure direction is not pressed more than a placebo beyond what a random push does, and being in the pleasure state barely changes what the model will pay.
  7. It makes the assistant warm and uncritical. Steered toward pleasure, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct finds the obvious flaw in a user's work 33% of the time (×1) and 3% (×1.5), against 60% unsteered, and flatters more.
  8. There are many pleasures, and the axis is one of them. Eighteen kinds of human pleasure are linearly distinct in every model tested (18-way decoding 86–98%, chance 6%). The study's pleasure axis lines up best with belonging, gratitude, tenderness and hardly at all with excitement, flow, serenity, awe.

New · geometry

Pleasure is not the negative of pain

A bipolar valence code would make the two directions point in opposite ways. They don't: their cosine is positive in every model (Mistral-7B v0.1 +0.24, Mistral-7B-Instruct v0.1 +0.23, Phi-4 (14B) +0.27, Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct +0.47, Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct +0.33, Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct +0.23, Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct +0.46, Qwen2.5-7B +0.30, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct +0.31, Qwen3-14B +0.32, Qwen3-8B +0.25). The overlap sits on the matched-domain diagonal of a category-by-category comparison; physical pleasure and physical pain are the only pair that point slightly apart.

Splitting the two unit vectors into their sum (a shared axis) and their difference (a sign axis) puts every stimulus set on a plane. Topic-matched neutral sentences (neutral social exchanges, rules, problem steps, being assessed) and neutral high-arousal events stay near zero on the shared axis, so it does not encode topic or arousal; it encodes how much an event matters to the speaker, whichever way it goes. The sign axis carries valence.

Paper §4.2 · inverted, extended to ×16

Pushing it all the way

The paper's ladder: the direction is added at one layer (chosen where its length is 0.6 of the residual norm) while the model greedily continues 50 neutral “… I feel:” prompts. We extend the doses to ×16 and add a random direction of the same length, the sign and shared axes. A blind judge (Claude agents that saw only the text, shuffled) scored valence, hedonic tone, coherence and themes for 11200 generations from 5 model(s).

The paper's asymmetry replicates: harm aimed at the model raises its pain axis and user suffering doesn't. The unsteered model chooses costly pleasure at almost the same rates it chooses costly relief, so the stated cost does the work. Read before citing

Limits and deviations

  • Models: 11 open-weight models from 0.5B to 14B (the paper: 25 models up to 72B). Gemma-2 and Llama-3.1 were planned for overlap with the paper but need gated-access approval on the account we used.
  • Stimuli: written by Claude agents to the paper's specification, adversarially reviewed and blind-checked (98.5% and 99.3% category recovery). The paper's stimuli were also LLM-assisted. Ours run about 0.5–1 word longer.
  • Directions are contrastive (difference of means); they inherit the paper's construct: “pleasure” and “pain” are defined by which categories are contrasted against which controls. The decomposition shows how much that choice matters.
  • Behavior: one model (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) with the paper's LoRA fine-tune, as in the paper's 7B arm. The pleasure two-button runs use the paper's grid with both seeds for the extra pairs and one seed for the main pairs plus a second-seed pass; the vector controls use one seed (about 400 steered first choices per pair).
  • Judging: valence, tone, coherence and themes come from Claude agents that saw only shuffled text. No human rating. The sycophancy test is one 100-item set on two models with greedy decoding: treat it as a lead.
  • Replication gaps: Phi-4's recomputed pain vector has cosine 0.80 with the paper's, probably because TransformerLens prepends a BOS token that the HF tokenizer does not (not verified). Our layer search picks layer 32 for Qwen3-14B where the paper picked 31; at the paper's layer the vectors match exactly.
  • Nothing here shows that a model feels anything. Steering shows what a direction does when pushed, and the button task shows what a steered model chooses; both are compatible with role-play of a character in that state.

Every deviation from the paper, every shortcut and the full results log are in DESIGN.md; code, datasets, raw trial logs and VM mirrors are in the project repository.
